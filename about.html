About Moveo

Our Mission

Moveo was created to make learning how to exercise less intimidating, more inclusive, and most of all, more visually engaging. We believe everyone deserves clear, non-judgmental fitness resources that help them move safely and confidently.

The Problem We're Solving

According to the World Health Organization (2023), over 1.8 billion adults globally are physically inactive, which increases the risk of chronic diseases and early mortality. A 2024 Statista survey found that roughly 58% of adults in the US want to exercise more but feel too intimidated to start.

Our Solution

Moveo presents exercises through light-hearted animations that make fitness feel less intimidating. By using visual storytelling instead of intimidating live-action videos, we lower the emotional barrier to starting fitness.

How to choose an exercise type

In the exercise library, filters describe movement roles (strength, mobility, rehab), not your long-term program goal. Hypertrophy and endurance are applied to workouts and prebuilt routines (sets, reps, rest, and session design), where those adaptations are mainly programmed.

Different training emphases create different adaptations. Match what you do today to the adaptation you want, then stay consistent long enough for the change to show up.

Strength

Choose strength when you want to increase how much force you can produce and build skills that carry over to everyday tasks. Most guidelines emphasize training major muscle groups multiple days per week and progressing load over time. This is also a strong foundation for long-term joint support and overall fitness. Sources: ACSM resistance training position stand (2026) and ACSM exercise prescription guidance.

Hypertrophy

Choose hypertrophy when your goal is to build muscle size. Muscle growth is driven by mechanical tension and training volume, and it responds well to multiple sets across the week with progressive overload. Sources: ACSM resistance training position stand (2026); Schoenfeld review on hypertrophy mechanisms (2010).

Endurance

Choose endurance when you want better stamina, heart and lung fitness, and improved ability to sustain effort. Public health guidance commonly recommends about 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity (or an equivalent mix), with more providing additional benefits. Sources: WHO physical activity guidelines (2020); ACSM quantity and quality of exercise position stand (2011).

Mobility

Choose mobility when you want to improve comfortable range of motion, movement quality, and how you feel during other training. Flexibility and mobility work is often recommended multiple days per week, with stretches held long enough to feel mild discomfort without pain. Sources: ACSM flexibility and stretching guidance.

Rehab

Choose rehab when you need a lower-risk path back to movement. Rehab-style work prioritizes control, a tolerable range, and gradual progression. For persistent pain patterns, graded exposure and graded exercise approaches are commonly used in physical therapy to reduce disability and fear of movement. Sources: evidence comparing graded exercise and graded exposure for chronic low back pain (e.g., RCTs and reviews).
